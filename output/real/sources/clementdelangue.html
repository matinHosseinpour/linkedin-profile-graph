    Once upon a time, a little Hug and a little Tractor made a promise to each other : go discover the world, and meet again in seven years, at the exact same place, to see who had the most adventurous adventures.

    The little Hug decided to set sail on the vast ocean, although everyone told her there was nothing to find out there. She was convinced that somewhere existed a source — an open source — where creation itself could originate from.

    The little Tractor, on the contrary, decided to stay on firm ground. He wanted to stay in touch with concrete reality. But he was determined to drive so far that he would reach the top of the world, something no little Tractor had done before him.

    For years, the little Hug seemed lost in infinite waters, without a direction. But every day, fishes, small and large, joined her and swam along as she sailed.

    For years, the little Tractor climbed. Despite early engine problems, and almost running out of fuel more times than he could count, he climbed one hill, then several, then real peaks.

    One dark night, pirates tried to board the little Hug’s boat. The storm lasted for days. She held the helm, and not a single fish swam away.

    A few days later, a mermaid approached her.
    “My name is Divina,” she said. “I love your boat, and I love all the fishes that follow you. I don’t think you should look any further for the source. I think you are the source.”

    Very far away, the little Tractor had reached the highest mountain he could find, and made friends with a unicorn who lived up there. Somehow, he was wondering what to do next.

    Seven years had passed, and the two friends met again to share their journeys. Comparing adventures, they realized nobody can ever claim to have seen it all — big stories or small ones, there’s always something to learn from a travelling friend. They promised not to wait another seven years next time. Then the little Hug went back to the sea to explore more unknown waters, and the little Tractor started his (now AI-powered) engine again : there were higher peaks to climb.

    18 of the 20 best open models today are Mixture-of-Experts. Dense is basically over.

    And yet the know-how to train a good MoE still lives mostly in the heads of a handful of teams.

    So we spent 800k GPU hours running the experiments ourselves and wrote everything down. Super happy to finally release "The Ultimate MoE Training Handbook", free and open.

    Quick refresher if you're not deep in this: an MoE splits each layer into many small "experts" and sends every token to only a few of them. You get a very large model for the compute cost of a much smaller one, which is why DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and most of the best open models went this way. The catch is that training one well is hard.

    The handbook is organized around the questions training teams actually hit:
    - how many experts, how big, shared or not?
    - why is half my step spent in all-to-all, and how do I hide it?
    - why is my run 25x slower with no error in the logs?
    - softmax, sigmoid or sqrt(softplus) router, and why keep it in fp32?
    - aux loss or DeepSeek's loss-free bias?
    - upcycle a dense checkpoint or train from scratch?
    - can I serve with fewer experts per token than I trained with?

    ⚠️ Fair warning: it's long (~70k words, budget a few days, sorry), but with 55 interactive figures, code you can run and numbers from our own runs.
